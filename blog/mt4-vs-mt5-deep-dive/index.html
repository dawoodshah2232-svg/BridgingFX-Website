MT4 vs MT5: The Deep Dive Most Comparisons Skip

Netting versus hedging, 32-bit versus 64-bit, MQL4 versus MQL5 — the technical differences that actually change how your brokerage runs day to day.

BridgingFX editorial · 2026-09-15

The MT4 versus MT5 debate has been running for over a decade, and most of what's written about it is a feature table copied from a broker comparison site. But if you're choosing the platform your brokerage will live on, the brochure features are the least interesting part. The real differences show up in how your dealing desk works, how your liquidity connects, what your developers can automate, and where your traders quietly get frustrated. Here's what actually separates the two, after years of deploying and operating both.

The architecture gap nobody explains

MT4 is 32-bit, single-threaded at its core, and built around a tick model that made perfect sense in 2005. MT5 is 64-bit, multi-threaded, and built around a position model designed for exchange-style trading. This isn't trivia — it determines how the platform behaves under load. An MT5 server handling ten thousand concurrent traders distributes work across cores in ways MT4 simply can't, and that shows up during the news spikes that matter most.

For a broker, the practical consequence is headroom. MT4 white labels scale by adding servers earlier and partitioning client groups more aggressively. MT5 scales more gracefully per server. If you're planning for growth rather than a static book, the architecture difference is worth more than any single feature comparison.

Netting vs hedging: the model that changes everything

MT5's default netting model — one position per symbol, volumes merged — is how exchanges work, and it's how most of the world's regulated markets operate. MT4's hedging model lets traders hold simultaneous long and short positions on the same symbol. Many retail traders have built entire strategies around hedging, and migrating them to netting feels like losing a feature.

Here's what brokers miss: MT5 does support hedging accounts — it's a per-account setting, not a platform limitation. The real operational difference is in how your risk system reads exposure. Netting gives you cleaner net exposure per symbol, which simplifies B-book risk calculations and A-book hedging. Hedging keeps traders comfortable but multiplies position counts and complicates your book. Decide the account model before you decide the platform version.

MQL4 vs MQL5: the ecosystem reality

MT4's EA ecosystem is the largest in retail trading history — hundreds of thousands of expert advisors, indicators, and scripts, plus a decade of forum knowledge. Traders arrive with their tools and expect them to work. That installed base is MT4's true moat, and it's the single strongest argument for launching on MT4.

MQL5 is objectively the better language — object-oriented, faster backtesting with real tick data and multi-currency testing, a proper strategy tester agent network. But 'better' doesn't move traders. What moves the needle for brokers is that MQL5's testing accuracy reduces the support load from 'my EA behaves differently on live' complaints, which are a genuine operational cost on MT4.

Liquidity, bridging, and the back office

Both platforms connect to liquidity through bridges, but MT5's gateway architecture was designed with institutional connectivity in mind — FIX API access, deeper market depth handling, and native support for multiple liquidity feeds per symbol. MT4 bridging works fine and the bridge market is mature, but you're bolting modern plumbing onto an older house.

Day to day, MT5's manager terminal and administrator give compliance and risk teams better tooling: more granular permissions, richer reporting, and audit trails that hold up better under regulatory scrutiny. If your jurisdiction's reporting requirements are tightening — and they are, nearly everywhere — this matters more than it did five years ago.

So which one do you actually choose?

Choose MT4 if your acquisition model depends on traders bringing their existing EAs and indicator stacks — affiliate-driven retail, EA marketplaces, signal-copy communities. The familiarity premium is real and measurable in conversion.

Choose MT5 if you're building for scale, serving stock/CFD multi-asset traders, operating under stricter reporting regimes, or planning a future where hedging-vs-netting doesn't define your product. And remember the migration question in reverse: moving traders from MT4 to MT5 later is a project, not a toggle — account models, EAs, and trader habits all have to move. It's cheaper to choose deliberately now than to migrate under pressure later.

The takeaway

MT4's moat is its ecosystem and trader familiarity; MT5's is architecture, scalability, and operational tooling. Choose from your trader backward and your five-year plan forward — and whatever you choose, make sure your back office doesn't lock you into a migration you could have avoided.
